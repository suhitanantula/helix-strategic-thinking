# Face 6: The AI Fog — We're Unsure How to Use AI in Strategy

## Overview

The tangled thoughts and branching possibilities when someone mentions "AI strategy." Leaders stand at the intersection of excitement and anxiety, knowing they need to act but unsure which direction leads to breakthrough versus breakdown. Pilots multiply but never connect. AI becomes a buzzword on slides rather than a force multiplier in practice.

**Rhythm Signature:** 〰●▲? — disconnected capabilities without integration

**Category:** Technological Disconnection

**Quote:** *"We're unsure how to use AI in strategy."*

MIT Sloan research confirms: organisations acknowledging uncertainty and adopting experimental approaches achieve **6x better financial outcomes** than those pursuing grand, top-down AI strategies.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when your organisation shows any of these patterns:

- AI initiatives proliferate but never connect to strategic objectives
- Teams over-index on AI experts while under-involving the people who'll actually use the tools
- AI is framed as a technology decision, not a strategic design question
- Leadership demands an "AI strategy" but can't articulate what success looks like
- The organisation moves either too fast (deploying without testing) or too vaguely (studying without acting)
- AI pilots succeed in isolation but fail to scale or integrate

---

## Root Cause

AI uncertainty emerges from three interlocking dysfunctions:

1. **Teams over-index on experts and under-involve users** — AI strategy becomes a specialist conversation rather than an organisational capability
2. **AI framed as a technology decision rather than a strategic design question** — The question isn't "What AI should we buy?" but "How might AI change our rhythm of creating value?"
3. **Moving too fast or too vaguely without rhythm** — Either deploying without learning or analysing without acting

It's not a tech issue — it's a **learning issue** and a **co-creation opportunity**. The rhythm dysfunction is 〰●▲? — capabilities exist but are disconnected, operating in silos without strategic integration.

---

## Diagnostic Questions

Ask these to assess whether your organisation is lost in The AI Fog:

1. **How might AI change your organisation's rhythm — not just its outputs?** — If the answer only mentions automation, you're thinking too small.
2. **Where in your strategic work could AI create the most meaningful acceleration, connection, or amplification?** — This maps AI potential to strategic rhythm.
3. **What's one small experiment you could run tomorrow to start building your AI strategic muscle?** — If nothing comes to mind, you lack experimentation infrastructure.
4. **Are you framing AI as a technology decision or a strategic design question?** — The framing determines the outcome.

---

## Solution: Lean Strategy (Ries) + Co-Intelligence (Mollick)

Shift from intimidation to iteration, from control to co-creation. The natural pattern is:

**● Loops** (experimentation) → **〰 Vibes** (intuitive understanding) → **▲ Lines** (structured action)

Treat AI ideas as hypotheses. Test early and small. Focus on value creation. Always invite AI to the table while keeping humans in the loop.

### The Lean Strategy + Co-Intelligence Framework

**Start with hypotheses, not blueprints.** Don't build a grand AI strategy. Instead, identify 3–5 areas where AI could accelerate, connect, or amplify strategic work. Frame each as a testable hypothesis.

**Test early, small, and fast.** Use Capital One's "stairway to heaven" methodology:
- **Loops:** 10-person pilots for months — deep learning about what works
- **Vibes:** Hundreds of users for weeks — intuitive understanding at scale
- **Lines:** Thousands before enterprise deployment — structured, proven action

**Focus on value creation, not technology adoption.** Every AI experiment should answer: "What strategic value does this create that we couldn't create before?"

**Always invite AI to the table.** Mollick's co-intelligence principle: AI is a thought partner, not just a tool. Include it in brainstorming, analysis, and evaluation — not just execution.

**Always keep humans in the loop.** Strategic judgment, ethical evaluation, and creative leaps remain human territory. AI amplifies; humans decide.

### SAFE Activation

**Ambiguity (A) + Co-Creation (S)** — Embrace the uncertainty of AI as a learning opportunity. Co-create AI strategy with the people who will use it, not just the people who understand the technology.

### Strategy Principle

**Strategy as Learning** — We don't know the best use of AI for our context until we try. Strategy is no longer "build and execute" but "try, learn, adjust, repeat." Grounded in Lean Startup, Co-Intelligence, and Agile experimentation.

## Practice: AI Strategy Co-Creation Lab

A structured workshop to move from AI fog to AI clarity:

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Rhythm Check** | 15 min | Where are we now? Map current AI maturity and rhythm gaps |
| **Possibility Space** | 30 min | Map AI possibilities across all functions |
| **Strategic Selection** | 20 min | Choose 3–5 highest-potential use cases |
| **Rhythm Design** | 30 min | Design the right rhythm for each use case (●〰▲) |
| **EEE Assessment** | 30 min | Ethical, Emotional, Emergent evaluation of each case |
| **Integration Plan** | 30 min | How does this connect to existing strategy? |
| **First Move** | 15 min | What experiment do we run tomorrow? |

**Total:** ~2.5 hours. Run once to kick off, then monthly for iteration.

## AI Integration

**Model:** Human-Led Strategic Direction → AI-Augmented Exploration → Human-AI Collaborative Evaluation → Human-Owned Implementation

### AI Roles

- **AI as Strategic Accelerant** — Generate and test multiple AI use cases in days, rapidly simulate ROI, compress learning cycles from quarters to weeks
- **AI as Strategic Connector** — Bridge gaps between technical and business teams, link customer pain points to AI solutions, translate priorities into capabilities
- **AI as Strategic Amplifier** — Extend imagination beyond conventional applications, surface non-obvious patterns, challenge assumed limitations

### Real-World AI Examples

- **Stitch Fix:** 〰●▲ multi-rhythm AI implementation — AI suggestions → human stylists → clients. $3.2B revenue (2024), 75% AI-driven recommendations
- **JPMorgan:** 2,000+ AI specialists, 300+ AI/ML use cases, $1.5B+ business value from AI/ML
- **Capital One:** "Stairway to heaven" methodology deployed AI to 20,000+ customer service agents
- **Nestlé:** NesGPT across 189 countries, 30% reduction in forecasting errors, product ideation from 6 months to 6 weeks
- **Johns Hopkins APL "Level Up":** 1,500+ employees, 10,000+ training sessions through gamified voluntary learning

### Key AI Prompts for This Face

1. "Analyse our current business model and identify the top 5 operational areas where AI could create the most strategic value within 6 months"
2. "Generate 10 creative ways we could use AI to solve our [specific challenge], ranging from simple augmentation to radical reinvention"
3. "What are the most likely unintended consequences of implementing AI in our [function]? Include second-order effects and ethical considerations"

**Reported Impact:** MIT SMR-BCG research shows experimental approaches achieve 6x better financial outcomes. Power users save 30+ minutes daily. Stitch Fix generates 10,000 product descriptions every 30 minutes.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] AI use cases are framed as testable hypotheses, not technology mandates
- [ ] At least 3 AI experiments are running with clear success metrics
- [ ] AI strategy involves the people who will use the tools, not just AI experts
- [ ] Every AI pilot has a defined path from experiment to integration
- [ ] EEE (Ethical, Emotional, Emergent) evaluation completed for each AI initiative
- [ ] AI is treated as a co-intelligence partner, not just an automation tool
- [ ] Learning from AI experiments is documented and shared across teams

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- **AI as silver bullet** — Believing AI will solve strategic problems without human judgment and organisational change
- **Pilot purgatory** — Running endless experiments that never connect to strategy or scale
- **Expert capture** — Letting AI specialists own AI strategy instead of co-creating with business teams
- **Big bang AI strategy** — Trying to build a grand AI blueprint instead of starting with hypotheses
- **Ignoring the rhythm** — Applying the wrong rhythm (▲ planning) when ● experimentation and 〰 sensing are needed
