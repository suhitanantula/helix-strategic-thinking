# Face 7: The Safety Dance — We Want Innovation But Keep Playing It Safe

## Overview

The innovation paradox: the desire to do something bold but the instinct to de-risk it into oblivion. Approval for experiments comes with the pressure to get it right the first time. The language of innovation fills slide decks while the delivery is business as usual. Innovation theatre replaces innovation courage.

**Rhythm Signature:** ▲▲▲ — false security through familiar patterns

**Category:** Cultural Risk Aversion

**Quote:** *"We want innovation but keep playing it safe."*

Companies implementing systematic innovation courage achieve **200% faster growth, 33% higher profitability, and 10x better ROI** than those trapped in innovation theatre.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when your organisation shows any of these patterns:

- Innovation is talked about constantly but nothing genuinely new ever ships
- Bold ideas get approved, then progressively de-risked until they're incremental
- "Innovation teams" exist but are isolated from core operations
- The organisation punishes failed experiments, creating a culture of safe bets
- Innovation is performative — hackathons, ideation sessions, innovation labs — but outputs don't change the business
- Quarterly pressure kills anything that won't show results within 90 days
- "Innovations" are genuinely just variations of what you've always done

---

## Root Cause

Two interlocking failures drive The Safety Dance:

1. **A 〰 courage gap** — Cultural risk aversion, fear of failure, and psychological unsafety. Research shows 70–90% of failures are blamed when only 1–4% are truly blameworthy. The cost of perceived failure is too high, so no one takes real risks.

2. **A ▲ systems gap** — Innovation theatre driven by structural misalignment: quarterly pressure killing long-term investment, surface activities without structural change, and innovation teams that are structurally separated from the core business.

The rhythm dysfunction is ▲▲▲ — stuck in familiar Lines with brief detours into ● Loops but always returning to what's safe. Innovation lives in the **〰●▲ flow between rhythms**, but the organisation can't sustain that flow. It keeps defaulting back to the safety of what it knows.

---

## Diagnostic Questions

Ask these to assess whether your organisation is trapped in The Safety Dance:

1. **What's the bold idea everyone knows but no one's spoken aloud?** — If this question makes people uncomfortable, you have a courage gap.
2. **What rhythm is your innovation process stuck in? (Lines / Loops / Vibes)** — If the answer is "Lines," your innovation is over-structured. If "Loops," you're iterating on the familiar. You need all three rhythms in concert.
3. **What's one small move you could make this week to shift from innovation theatre to innovation courage?** — If nothing comes to mind, the system is constraining you.
4. **Are your 'innovations' genuinely new or variations of what you've always done?** — Honest assessment: are you innovating or redecorating?

## Solution: Platform Strategy + Innovation Portfolio

Move from ▲▲▲ innovation theatre → **〰●▲ innovation courage** through systematic transformation.

### Platform Strategy

Think in **networks and value exchange**, not just products. Leverage others' capabilities, design for scalability, and engage with the larger ecosystem of value — users, partners, platforms, adjacent markets. True innovation comes from the ecosystem, not internal brainstorming alone.

### Innovation Portfolio

Balance risk across three horizons:

- **Horizon 1 (70%) — Core:** Optimise what exists. Incremental innovation on proven products and services.
- **Horizon 2 (20%) — Adjacent:** Extend into new markets, customers, or capabilities. Measured risk with clear learning goals.
- **Horizon 3 (10%) — Transformational:** Bold bets on entirely new possibilities. High risk, high reward, long time horizons.

### Supporting Layers

**EEE Evaluation** — Every innovation concept passes through three lenses:
- **Ethical:** Does this create value responsibly?
- **Emotional:** Does this resonate with humans?
- **Emergent:** Does this open possibilities we can't yet see?

**Multi-Rhythm Innovation** — Match the rhythm to the type:
- **▲ Lines** for structured, measurable innovation (Horizon 1)
- **● Loops** for iterative, experimental innovation (Horizon 2)
- **〰 Vibes** for emergent, creative innovation (Horizon 3)

### SAFE Activation

**Futures (F) + Emergence (E)** — Build strategic foresight capabilities. Design conditions for innovation to emerge rather than planning it into submission.

### Strategy Principle

**Strategy as Ecosystem** — True innovation comes from engaging with the larger system of value (users, partners, platforms, adjacent markets), not internal brainstorming alone. Design for emergent value creation, not just core capability extension.

## Practice: Innovation Portfolio Workshop

A structured workshop to move from safety to courage:

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **Rhythm Assessment** | 20 min | Map your current innovation rhythms — where are you stuck? |
| **Innovation Inventory** | 30 min | Audit all current initiatives across 3 horizons |
| **Courage Gap Analysis** | 20 min | Where are bold ideas getting de-risked into nothing? |
| **AI-Enhanced Ideation** | 30 min | Generate genuinely new concepts using AI as creative partner |
| **EEE Evaluation** | 30 min | Ethical, Emotional, Emergent assessment of each concept |
| **Portfolio Optimisation** | 20 min | Balance horizons; identify over- and under-invested areas |
| **System Design** | 20 min | What structural changes enable courage? |

**Total:** ~2.5 hours. Run quarterly to keep the portfolio balanced and honest.

## Real-World Examples

### Failure: The Safety Dance Destroys

- **Kodak (1975–2012)** — Invented the digital camera in 1975, accumulated 7,000+ patents, refused to cannibalise film business. Bankruptcy in 2012. Lost 90% market share to disruption they pioneered.
- **GE Digital (2013–2018)** — $4B+ invested in Predix platform, scaled without product-market fit. The safety of "we're investing in innovation" without the courage to validate.
- **Nokia (2007–2013)** — Developed touchscreen prototypes internally but dismissed them as non-core. 40% market share collapsed to 1%.

### Success: The Safety Dance Overcome

- **Microsoft under Nadella (2014–present)** — From "know-it-all" to "learn-it-all" culture. $300B to $3T+ market cap. Cultural courage as strategic transformation.
- **Adobe Creative Cloud (2012)** — Risked entire business model shifting from perpetual licenses to subscriptions. Revenue from $4.2B to $19B+, 10x stock price. The courage to cannibalise your own success.
- **3M's 15% Time** — Untracked personal project time generated Post-It Notes, N95 masks, and 22,800+ patents. Systematic courage embedded in structure.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Innovation portfolio is balanced across all three horizons (H1/H2/H3)
- [ ] Failed experiments are celebrated as learning, not punished as failures
- [ ] At least one genuinely bold initiative is in active development (not just incremental)
- [ ] EEE evaluation applied to every innovation concept
- [ ] Innovation teams are integrated with core operations (not isolated skunkworks)
- [ ] AI is used to expand creative possibility space, not just optimise existing processes
- [ ] Quarterly pressure does not kill initiatives with longer than 90-day horizons

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Innovation theatre** — Hackathons, labs, and ideation sessions that produce slides but not shipped products
- **De-risking into nothing** — Taking bold ideas and progressively making them safe until they're indistinguishable from the status quo
- **Isolation of innovation** — Creating separate innovation teams that never influence core operations
- **Horizon 1 bias** — Allocating 95%+ of resources to incremental improvements while calling it innovation
- **Punishing failure** — Creating an environment where failed experiments end careers rather than build capability
